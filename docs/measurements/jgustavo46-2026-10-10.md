# jgustavo46 — first measurements (10/10/2026)

Host: AMD Radeon RX 480 (Polaris, `radeonsi`), 16 cores, 32 GB, kernel 6.12.107, no binderfs (legacy binder with 12
slots). Official Redroid 15 image, redroid-forge at `8d6f7e9`. Taken with the scenario runner
([docs/SCENARIOS.md](../SCENARIOS.md)). **One run each, not repeated yet**: the min/max of the decode runs inside a
scenario give an idea of the spread, but treat the figures as a first reading, not a benchmark.

## Decode: hardware against software (1 instance)

H.264 High, 1080p30, B-frames and 4 references (generated with ffmpeg), decoded flat out for 30 s through MediaCodec
(`hwdec_mediacodec_test`, which also computes the CRC of every frame, so read the figures as a comparison, not as player
performance).

| | hardware (`c2.hardware.decoder.h264`, VA-API) | software (`OMX.google.h264.decoder`) |
|---|---|---|
| speed | **138.6 fps** (136.5 – 140.3, 12 runs) | 95.1 fps (82 – 109.1, 9 runs) |
| CPU of the instance, mean / max | **1.0** / 1.4 cores | 2.8 / 3.8 cores |
| CPU per decoded frame | ~0.007 core·s | ~0.029 core·s |
| host CPU, mean | 11.8 % | 21.9 % |
| GPU power, mean | 39.4 W | 17.6 W |
| UVD (decode engine) powered up | 89 % of the samples | 0 % |

The hardware decoder is **1.5× faster and uses about 4× less CPU per frame**; the video decode engine (UVD) was really
working in the hardware run and idle in the software one. The price is on the GPU side: about 22 W more than with the
software decoder.

## Cost of an instance

- An instance with hwenc at rest: **0.05 cores and ~1.1 GB**, boots in ~10 s.
- 12 instances at once (small screens, no hwenc): ~950 MB and ~0.4 % CPU each when idle, all booted in 35 s; one of them
  restarted once during the burst of simultaneous boots and recovered by itself (the load average reached 12 on 16
  cores). Staggering the creation avoids it. (Taken with the API and `docker stats`, before the runner existed.)

## Encode (1 instance, `screenrecord` over a moving 1280×720 screen, 30 fps)

| asked | delivered | the daemon's own report |
|---|---|---|
| 8 Mbps | **4.94 Mbps**, 30 fps, valid H.264 | target 8.00 Mbps, **QP 14 = its quality floor in 100 % of the windows** |
| 2 Mbps | **2.35 Mbps**, 30 fps, valid H.264 | target 2.49 Mbps, QP 28–31 (not at the floor: it is steering) |

- **The rate control of PR #19 works on the Polaris.** With a low target it raises the QP and lands within 6 % of what it
  was asked (2.35 of 2.49); with a high target it spends bits until its quality floor (QP 14) and no further: this
  content (a scrolling list) simply does not cost more than ~5 Mbps at that quality. To make the encoder reach 8 Mbps
  the content must be more complex (video, 3D): the next phase.
- The 2.49 Mbps the daemon was asked for is **not** what `screenrecord` asked (2.00): it matches exactly 1280 × 720 × 30 ×
  0.09, a minimum number of bits per pixel that Android appears to enforce before the request reaches the encoder (read
  from the numbers; not checked in the AOSP source).
- The **VCE engine** was powered up in 76 % of the samples, with no kernel errors.

Before this, two runs delivered ~2.9 Mbps whatever was asked, with `target 0.00 Mbps` in the daemon's report: the
hwenc artifacts of this host were older than the rate control and the component did not send the bitrate. They were
rebuilt from `main` (`android/ninja.sh`, 15 s, no Soong) and copied to the host; the runner now detects that case by
itself and checks that the artifacts are newer than the component's source.
